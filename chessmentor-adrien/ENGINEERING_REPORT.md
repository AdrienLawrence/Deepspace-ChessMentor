## Decisions

`chess.js` supplies deterministic rules, not opponent intelligence. Full replay preserves repetition detection. OpenAI selects legal candidate moves and provides educational explanations; there is no chess engine or fabricated evaluation score.

The existing DeepSpace RecordRoom remains the persistence and synchronization layer. An internal command handler runs all chess writes through one per-owner queue within that room, and validates revisions before each commit. Client-side selection and historical navigation never become authoritative game state.

Messages are persisted in pending/completed/failed states, with deterministic request IDs. Validated final responses are delivered without token streaming. Feedback is cached once per game/ply and generated by the open live workspace. Durable background inference is outside this implementation.

## Insufficient-material investigation

The existing authoritative `outcome()` already calls chess.js insufficient-material detection after every committed move, including promotions and opponent moves. No rule rewrite was necessary. Added 14 regression cases: lone bishops/knights belonging to either side; White and Black underpromotions; positions with additional material that must remain active; and a complete legal game replay ending in either a knight or bishop promotion, tested for both player and opponent actors. The committed game immediately becomes `finished`, result `1/2-1/2`, reason `Insufficient material`, and further moves are rejected. The inspected live game had promoted to a queen, so its active status was expected. Tests use a local replay and do not change that saved game.
